/ 05 — product principles

built for the shift, not the pitch.

The strongest POS experience is not the one with the most panels. It is the one people can use confidently, repeatedly, on a busy day.

01

clear before clever.

Prioritise legible prices, obvious actions, and predictable navigation over decorative complexity.

02

fast paths for repeat work.

Keep common tasks close at hand and reduce avoidable steps in frequently used flows.

03

useful information, not noise.

Surface the operational details that help an owner or operator make the next decision.

04

honest about the build.

Show only the workflows, integrations, and reliability guarantees the product can currently deliver.

/ 06 — useful questions

before you
put it to work.

A few practical questions to answer before inviting a business to use the product.

is this ready for every kind of business?

It is being shaped as a flexible POS foundation. The right fit depends on the products or services you sell and the workflows supported by the current build.

does it handle inventory as well as billing?

The product direction includes billing and inventory in one workflow. Before launch, specify exactly which stock actions are live today — such as adjustments, low-stock alerts, variants, and returns.

can i use it for my existing store process?

The best next step is to map your checkout, catalogue, and reporting needs against the working prototype. Avoid promising migration or customisation until those paths are tested.

is the product available to use now?

This page presents a working-prototype stage product. Replace this answer with your actual access status, onboarding process, and supported environment before publishing.
